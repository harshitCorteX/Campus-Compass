03 / A*

Explore with an estimate.

Combine the cost so far with a lower-bound estimate to the goal. Expand the state with the smallest f.

f(n) = g(n) + h(n)
For your viva: environment, costs & heuristic +

Environment classification

Fully observable: the complete modeled graph and conditions are known.
Deterministic: an action has a known destination and fixed current cost.
Sequential: each move affects the next choices.
Dynamic: conditions can change between searches, triggering replanning.

What are we minimizing?

Distance: d metres.
Time: (d / 80) × (1 + 2c) minutes.
Accessibility: distance, excluding non-accessible edges.
Low crowd: d × (1 + 3c) weighted metres.

d = distance; c = crowd level from 0 to 1. These are demonstration assumptions.

Why is A* optimal here?

h(n) is straight-line map distance to the goal, multiplied by the smallest allowed edge cost / geometric length ratio. This makes the heuristic consistent and admissible, even on a schematic map.

Each search uses a frozen snapshot. Travel estimates and accessibility flags are sample data, not live campus guidance.

YOUR 5-MINUTE DEMONSTRATION

Let the campus tell the story.

  1. Start with the graph.

    Explain that the 16 locations are states and the 25 paths are actions.

  2. Run the shortest-route demo.

    Choose A*, play the trace, and explain g, h, and f using the open list.

  3. Compare BFS and DFS.

    Use the comparison table to discuss segments, weighted costs, and expansion order.

  4. Block a path.

    Choose demo 02. The agent replans around a closed path, demonstrating a dynamic environment.

  5. Try step-free access.

    Choose demo 03 and show how inaccessible edges are excluded before searching.
